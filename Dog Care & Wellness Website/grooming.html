Dog Grooming

Regular grooming helps keep dogs clean and comfortable while also giving owners a chance to notice changes in their dog's skin, coat, ears, and nails. Grooming needs can vary depending on the dog's breed, coat type, age, and lifestyle.

Bathing & Brushing

Brushing helps remove loose hair, dirt, and tangles while keeping the coat looking healthy. How often a dog needs to be brushed depends on the type and length of its coat.

Dogs should be bathed when needed using products made for dogs. Bathing too frequently may dry out the skin and coat.

Nail Care

Keeping a dog's nails at a comfortable length can make walking easier and help prevent nails from becoming overgrown. Nails should be checked regularly and trimmed carefully when needed.

If you are uncomfortable trimming your dog's nails, a veterinarian or professional groomer can help.

Ear Cleaning

Checking your dog's ears regularly can help you notice dirt, unusual odor, redness, or irritation. Some dogs may need their ears cleaned more often than others.

Use an ear-cleaning product intended for dogs and avoid placing objects deep inside the ear canal.

Coat Care

A dog's coat can provide clues about overall health. Regular grooming can help owners notice excessive shedding, dry skin, lumps, parasites, or other changes that might otherwise be missed.

Contact a veterinarian if you notice persistent skin irritation, unusual hair loss, painful ears, swelling, or other concerning changes during grooming.
